# ChrysaLisp 7.3

The first release of the Storage system. The machines of a mesh keep one
store between them, each block on three of them, and a program reads and
writes it as it would a file, or sends a function out to where the blocks
are and has the answer back. With it a band of the scheduler for tasks
that work by themselves for others, textures in the shader language, a
Whiteboard made over, and the compiler and its tools a long way on.

This is a tag, not a GitHub release.

**Update with `make install`.** The host programs have new calls, a cheap
clock and the textures of the GPU, and the boot image needs them.

A draft, what has gone in since the 7.2 tag.

## Storage

*	A store kept by the machines of a mesh between them. An object is cut
	into blocks of 64KB, each named by its SHA-256, so a block never
	changes and two that are the same are one. A path names an object, and
	every member knows every path. Each block is kept by three members, as
	the members and its name say, with no table and nobody asked: when a
	member comes or goes only its share moves.
	[`docs/ai_digest/storage_service.md`](../ai_digest/storage_service.md).

*	`storage -j` makes a machine a member of the store of its mesh, and
	from then on its store starts by itself. The id of the store is made
	from `mesh_key`, so machines with the same key are of the same store.
	The store is `storage/store.img`, an exFAT volume in a file, 4GB at
	most, taking only what is put in it. The Storage app has a Join button.

*	The `storage` command puts, gets and lists, says who the members are,
	sets how many keep the blocks of a path, `-k`, pins a prefix to a
	member, which then holds all of what is under it, and takes snapshots.

*	`(storage-stream path [mode svc ahead])` reads, writes and appends an
	object as `(file-stream)` does a file, and reads ahead. A program
	that knows only streams uses the store with no change.
	`(lisp-stream fill [seek write])`, a stream whose bytes come from Lisp.

*	A gopher is a function sent to the blocks of an object. It is run
	where each block is, goes on to the next member with what it has
	worked out, and its answer comes back. A 37MB film is gone over in
	1.05s by a gopher, 1.1s read as a stream. 300 can be out at once, all
	right, in about 0.2s. `(storage-walk)`, and a library of them,
	`service/storage/gophers.inc`.

*	A gopher's function has 50ms for a block, and one that is not done in
	that goes to the back of the queue, a second runner that waits while
	the others have blocks to do, with a second. One that goes round for
	ever is ended by its own loops, and who sent it is told it is not
	coming back. Nothing is thrown.

*	`storage -z MB` puts a member's store on a volume of another size, a
	copy on the one machine, even one too full to write its own map.

*	A machine that is taken away, a laptop, `storage -L on`, holds all of
	its user's area, `usr/<user>/`, pinned as its store starts.

*	Snapshots, `storage -n name`, of the whole store or a prefix of it,
	put back with `-R` and dropped with `-D`. A snapshot is paths set to
	the same objects, no block is copied.

*	Two machines that wrote a path while apart keep both: one has the
	path, and the other is kept beside it with a `~` and part of its name.

*	A recorder. Each member says what it does, a line each, to whoever
	asks, `storage -r file`. And scenes, `storage -t name`: a store of
	its own, a thing done to it, a member cut off, stopped, a tree of
	files put, what happens said and checked. `storage -t` lists them.

*	The Storage app, under System, is a player of what the store does:
	the members, the blocks each keeps, the objects, and what was done, at
	any event of a recording or live. A timeline goes back and forth
	through it, a gopher's way across the members is drawn, and trace
	keeps to one object or one gopher. A recording is saved and opened,
	the same lines `storage -r` writes. Join and Travels are on its
	toolbar.

*	The times on an Apple M4 Max. Every file of the tree, 2014 files of
	162MB, put into one member in 6s and read back in 2s. Into five
	members by a farm in 3s, and every member has its share 13s after.
	Over the M4, an x86_64 Mac and a Raspberry Pi 4, 2035 files of 188MB
	were put in 144s and read back the same in 177s.

## Pictures, files and the web

*	PNG is read, every kind of pixel, `lib/image/png.inc`, and JPEG, baseline
	and progressive, grey and colour of any sampling, `lib/image/jpg.inc`.
	Each is a pipeline of tasks on its node, a stream to a stream each, as
	CPM is, so no whole frame is held between stages; a progressive JPEG
	keeps the numbers of its blocks till its last scan. The three, and the
	CPM saver, are a list of stages each given to `lib/streams/stages.inc`.
	A PNG's filters are undone, and its rows of 8 or 16 bits made pixels, by
	native code, `lib/image/lisp.vp`: a picture of 512 by 512 from 715ms to
	250ms on the M4, its unfilter stage from 467ms to 5ms.

*	`lib/streams/deflate.inc`, inflate, DEFLATE and zlib, a stream to a
	stream. `lib/files/zip.inc` reads a `.zip`.

*	LZ4: the frame says the size of block it has, and its header sum is
	right; the one before was wrong, and the `lz4` tool took nothing we
	wrote. A frame of blocks bigger than 64KB was read into a ring of 64KB,
	past its end. A frame of the `lz4` tool with the size of its content, a
	dictionary or sums of its blocks is read.

*	Huffman is no longer an experiment, `lib/streams/huffman.inc`:
	canonical codes no longer than a limit, kept as their lengths, those
	inflate reads by. `huff` codes a block at a time, a code of its own for
	each; with a book from `hbook`, which now has a code for every token,
	a token without one was dropped. The six commands of `lz4`, `rle` and
	`huff` read a file or stdin by `(options-input)`.

*	HTTPS. `lib/net/tls.inc` is a TLS 1.3 client, X25519 and
	ChaCha20-Poly1305, `lib/crypto/x25519.inc` and `lib/crypto/hkdf.inc`
	under it, and `https://` works in `http.inc` and `curl`. The server's
	certificates are checked, `lib/crypto/x509.inc`: up to a root of those
	the host keeps, `/etc/ssl/cert.pem` or the like, the first for the
	host asked, each in date, and the handshake signed by its key. ECDSA on
	P-256 and P-384, `lib/crypto/ecdsa.inc`, and RSA by PKCS #1 v1.5 and
	PSS, `lib/crypto/rsa.inc`, on big numbers of their own,
	`lib/math/bignum.inc`, with SHA-384 and Base64. One that fails is not
	talked to and `curl` says why; `curl -k` does not ask. A checked fetch
	of GitHub is 2s. A server that has only TLS 1.2, or only AES-GCM, is not
	reached yet.

*	New Model demo: a `.obj` with its `.mtl` and pictures, from a file, a
	`.zip`, or an `https://` address, turned with the mouse, on the GPU or
	native code. `lib/math/obj.inc` reads `.obj` and `.mtl`, and `Mesh-obj`
	is made on it.

## The scheduler

*	A band for bots, between the GUI and the apps, `(task-band :bot)`: a
	task that works by itself for others. The Storage service, its tender
	and its gophers are bots, so gophers on nodes busy with apps are not
	slowed, 16s to 0.24s. A bot rests every 20ms if an app is waiting,
	`(task-breathe)`, and all the bots of a node rest together: apps wait
	under 100ms more than they asked, with gophers that never come back.

*	`(task-slice)` asks a clock that is cheap to ask, a third off what it
	costs on an x86_64 Mac, a quarter on the Pi.

*	The iterators make the one list of args for a whole loop, not one each
	time round, 5 to 8ns off each time round of 10 to 20.

## vpx, the compiler of VP

*	C-Script is vpx, a real compiler, for VP code and only for VP code,
	never Lisp. An expression is a form now, `(vpx-compile)`: the text
	and its parser are gone. The files, the commands and the pages are of
	the new name, `docs/vm/vpx.md`, `docs/ai_digest/vpx_compiler.md`.

*	A method's declaration says what its function trashes,
	`(dec-method name function type in out trashes own)`, where it was a
	comment. `trace -l` says where a declaration is not what the code does
	and `trace -w` writes it. `:obj :destroy`, and so a deref, trashes
	three registers where it trashed fifteen, and 190 functions trash
	less for it.

*	Named locals are kept in registers in every function that has them,
	`(vpx-stack)` to have them on the stack. `lib/asm/vpxreg.inc`.

*	New `locals`: what a function's named locals cost, and with `-c` what
	each method called costs those that call it.

*	New `relies`: where code reads a register after a call that is
	declared to trash it, and with `-s`, a register saved round a call
	that leaves it. The lint runs it, and it is clean.

## The kernel and tasks

*	A task that sleeps finds its registers as they were.

*	An object 250 deep in others was the end of its task when let go of,
	and nothing was said. It is let go of at any depth.

*	The answer to an open says which node gives it, the task started or
	not, and `(. global :reply msg)` takes it: `(Global)` does not ask a
	node again that could not start its task.

*	The last `@` service of a node, forgotten, is forgotten by the other
	nodes of its machine too. Its name stayed in the Services app for ever.

*	A policy written down: a node need not be ChrysaLisp, the links are
	the surface. [`docs/ai_digest/alien_nodes.md`](../ai_digest/alien_nodes.md).

*	A machine with no `mesh_key` is given one by `make install`, and its
	Net service joins the mesh by itself, the first session on port 3333.
	A change of the key, or its removal, drops the links. `(net-drop)`.

*	A subnet can have a system id of its own, `nodes -s ring -o`.

*	`(pii-kill pid)`, and `(node-stop)` ends a node that is still there
	after 2.5 seconds. A task is not sent to a node that has gone, and a
	link sends on the mail it held for one.

*	`(mail-timeout mbox 0 0)` takes away every timeout of that mailbox.

*	Action ids 0 to 15 are the system's, `+event_user` is the first an app
	has.

*	The options library is in the root, a command starts in 67µs, it was
	98. `lib/math/matrix.inc`, the matrices and vectors of the plane.
	`(poly-hit?)` and `(poly-convex-hit?)`, do two shapes touch, native.
	`(import-cmd)`.

*	A view works on a node with no GUI.

## Tools

*	New `bench`, a suite of benchmarks in `tests/bench/`, `bench -s file`
	before a change and `bench -c file` after: the iterators, the
	evaluator, strs, maps, streams and the polygon fill.

*	`check` says what can be seen to be wrong from the source alone, more
	of it: brackets that do not pair, a constant used in its own `(defq)`,
	a str cut to a length it may not have, a constant list made at run
	time, `(const)` of a function made inside another, `(eql)` of a quoted
	list with a str or a list in it, a `(defq)` again of a name an inner
	function uses, and a function given to another that uses one of its
	names; and in a method of a class, a `(call)` of the same method of a
	class at or above it on `:r0`, which goes by the vtable to itself.

*	`(fill array length value)`, an array so long, each element the one
	given, of a list a reference to the object for each: the `:fill`
	methods of `:array` and `:list`.

*	`brackets` reads a `{}` string as code being made, its brackets pair
	by themselves across a name put into it. The tree is clean.

*	`symbols -p file [name ...]` draws the symbols as they are in
	`lib/font/symbol_set.inc`, in every theme, at the size of a toolbar
	and big, before the fonts are made.

*	The tokeniser of the highlighter is a function, `(tokenize-line)`, in
	`lib/text/tokenize.inc`.

*	`check`, it was `progns`, gives where an `(if)` of an `(and)` and one
	form is the `(and)`, a `(progn)` an else or a body does not need, and
	more: 242 places in 106 files were written so.

*	`forward` reads with Syntax, leaves comments alone, says where a
	function calls itself, and is a stage of the release.

*	`release`, `debug` and `validate` go with any make target, and `make
	vp` with none is release.

*	`diff` writes what diff(1) writes, and `patch`, which failed on every
	diff, works.

*	`shader -p` shows the other half of a pair. `./run.sh -u name` and
	`rack -u`, and `-a`, a session that does not join the mesh by itself.
	Ctrl-R searches what was typed, in the Terminal and the TUI.

## Whiteboard

*	Made over on documents of shapes. A shape is SVG path text and a
	matrix, in layers of groups, with ids, and every change can be undone.
	`.cwb` is version 4, and versions 2 and 3 are read and made 4.
	`lib/cwb/doc.inc`, `board.inc`, `pointer.inc` and `tools.inc`.

*	Pens, fingers and many touches at once come from the host as pointer
	events. A touch is the thing it lands on till it lifts. Two fingers on
	empty board move and zoom the view, a twist of them, or Ctrl-R, turns
	it a quarter.

*	The board is a plane with no edge, the paper a view of it, zoomed in
	stops each twice the last. Snap is to every line of the paper that
	shows. Full screen from max, from the palette, or Esc, and min fits
	the paper again.

*	A ring palette opens where it is tapped, and what a pen picks is that
	pen's.

*	The eraser bites a piece out of whatever it touches, lines too, and
	can be undone. The polygon set logic it rests on is native, 38.5ms to
	5.7ms over 8,700 points on the M4: `(poly-union)`, `(poly-intersect)`,
	`(poly-difference)`, `(poly-xor)`. [`docs/ai_digest/polygon_sets.md`](../ai_digest/polygon_sets.md).

*	The instruments: a ruler in cm and inches with the angle it is at, a
	half and a whole protractor, a set square of 30 and 60 and one of 45.
	Each turns, sizes and moves by zones of its own, is drawn to every
	45 degrees and every 10, and comes to the front when taken hold of.

*	A big board is drawn in strips by the nodes, 40,000 shapes in 42ms
	where one task takes 243. Group and delete of 12,000 shapes, 2,434ms
	to 8ms.

*	New `cwb` command, a board driven with no hand: make one, run Lisp on
	it, play pointer events at it, and draw it to a picture.
	`(TGA-save)`, and `(canvas-save)` takes `.tga`.

## Networks

*	A key for the links between machines. A machine with the file
	`mesh_key` links only to machines with the same one, each end proves it
	has it before the link carries anything. `link -k` makes a key. `sync`
	and everything else that goes over a link is behind it. With no key
	file nothing is changed. [`docs/intro/intro.md`](../intro/intro.md), "A Key".

*	`sync` sends the mode of a file, who may read, write and run it. A
	script arrives as one that can be run, and a file that is the same but
	for its mode has only that set. [`docs/ai_digest/sync.md`](../ai_digest/sync.md).

*	New `rack`, a command line run on every machine of the mesh. Each is
	made the same as this one, then runs it in a session of its own, `rack
	"tests -a"`. `./rack.sh up` makes a machine a member. It is how the
	system is tested on three machines at once. [`docs/ai_digest/rack.md`](../ai_digest/rack.md).

*	New `mesh` command, one command to join a machine to the others on a
	network, `mesh -j`, to make a key, `mesh -k`, and to say who is there
	and what to look at if nobody is. [`docs/intro/mesh.md`](../intro/mesh.md) is the guide.

*	A build has an id, the same on every CPU, and a machine says its own in
	its beacon. `mesh` names a machine that is on a different build.

*	New `lib/hash/tree.inc`, a tree of hashes. The hash of the top is of
	everything under it, and two trees are compared from the top down,
	only where they differ. `sync` is on it: two machines with the same
	files say one number each, and `sync` with no options says which
	machines have the same tree as this one. [`docs/ai_digest/sync.md`](../ai_digest/sync.md).

## Fixes

*	A line of text costs its length, not the square of it: the polygon
	fill keeps its edges in order with a merge. A label given 4,000
	characters stopped the desktop for 43 seconds, it is 0.08 now. A
	`Text` draws no more than the first 512 bytes of a line.

*	The Windows host: a listen starts the sockets if nothing has, and
	holds its port alone. Found with the Windows host run under Wine,
	where the install and every test pass.

*	The kernel did not build for LoongArch and RISC-V, the passes of the
	assembler never ended. A length that goes by a label only grows now,
	and the passes run till the output is the same.

*	A command that only sleeps, or only works and never reads or writes, is
	stopped when its pipe is aborted, Ctrl-Shift-D in the Terminal. It
	went on to the end.

*	A command that does not exist is "Pipe Error !", it hung the terminal.
	The kernel again looks that a `.lisp` file is there before it starts a
	task for it, a look lost on 8 September, in 7.1 and 7.2.

*	A command whose file throws as it loads says why in the terminal that
	ran it, and the prompt comes back. The error went to the host console
	and the terminal waited for ever.

*	`(path-filter)` into a path that is not the source makes room in it,
	it wrote past the end. `(path-simplify)` keeps the whole of a shape, it
	gave half.

*	The rules of a `.gitignore` in `sync`, a line with a space or a return
	on the end was not matched.

*	A pipe never put away the timer of its close: 5,000 `echo hi` grew
	from 331ms to 679ms in one task, and is a steady 288ms now.

*	Since 3 August two maps, two sets or two views with the same contents
	were equal, and the Todo app could not erase.

*	A fill with soft edges was drawn a pixel right and half a pixel low,
	and a stroke was narrower than asked. Every glyph has moved a little.

*	A node a session started held the session's sockets, so port 3333
	stayed taken after it.

*	A machine with two addresses dialled itself. A session could leave
	notes and link files in /tmp, and its notes could have a stranger's
	process killed.

*	A command's errors were lost from a pipe. A press past the end of a
	text field crashed it. A short SVG arc was up to 15 pixels out.
	`(structure)` with a `(union)` threw. `nodes -x` left nodes running.

*	The emulator shifts by the low 6 bits of the count, and a real made an
	integer goes towards 0 and stops at the most there is. LoongArch's
	`vp-lnot-rr` was wrong.

## Not ready

*	What goes over a link is not hidden or signed, the key says who is let
	in. That is so of the store too, its blocks go in the clear.

*	HTTPS speaks only TLS 1.3 with ChaCha20-Poly1305. Not checked of a
	certificate: whether it has been revoked, the names an issuer is
	limited to, and the uses its key is. A host with no roots where they
	are looked for, Windows, reaches no https server but with `curl -k`. JPEG is baseline only, not progressive, and PNG not
	interlaced.

*	The store: blocks that no object is made of are let go of only when
	asked, `storage -w`. How long a store over several machines takes
	till every block has its copies has not been timed. A written stream
	sets its path whoever else has set it since. What a gopher's function
	may do is not fenced. Stores are not yet mounted into one tree.

*	The Whiteboard has one selection a board, not one a person, and two
	mice are one device.

*	Windows: `funcs.ps1` has no `-u` or `-a`, `(node-stop)` of a name does
	nothing there, and `(pii-kill)` has not been run there.

*	`(n2i)` of a real too big for an integer differs by CPU.

*	Ed25519 is not done in constant time, and nothing uses it yet.
