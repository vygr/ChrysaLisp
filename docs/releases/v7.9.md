## Lisp

*	The XML parser reads XML 1.0 as it is written, still in one pass in
	VP, `lib/xml/lisp.vp`, now knowing where in the document it is: text
	is given whole, as it is, where it was split into words, and a word
	with `-`, `/`, `?`, `=` or a quote in it became markup and stopped
	the parse; `<b/>` with no space is an element `b`, not `b/` never
	closed; an apostrophe in text or a comment no longer swallows the
	rest of the file; CDATA is text; a DOCTYPE with `[ ]` is passed over;
	names with `:` are names. The five named entities and `&#n;` `&#xh;`,
	in values and text, are what they stand for, as UTF-8,
	`(xml-entities)`. `XML-parse`'s callbacks are as they were, the Lisp
	state machine behind them gone: the tokenizer gives each element
	opened, closed or empty, its attributes, and the text. The SVG
	reader's text is SVG's default, white space one space and none at
	the ends. The nine SVGs of the Images app draw the same to the byte;
	monroe.svg, 5MB, parses in 27ms where it took 32.

*	Gradients on a canvas: `(. canvas :set_linear x y x1 y1 stops
	[spread])`, t 0 at x y and 1 at x1 y1, and `(. canvas :set_radial cx
	cy r stops [spread])`, in the pixels `:fpoly` draws in. Every fill
	after it is of it, shapes and glyphs by `:fpoly`, boxes by `:fbox`,
	till `:set_color` puts the solid color back. The stops, `((offset
	argb) ...)`, are 256 colors, each channel mixed straight between the
	stops either side and not premultiplied, offsets kept to 0 to 1 and in
	order as SVG has them; the spread is `+spread_pad`, `+spread_reflect`
	or `+spread_repeat`. A linear gradient is exact: each run's first
	pixel is worked out from integers of the fixed inputs, its step of the
	256 carried along by quotient and remainder, as `:ftri` did its edges,
	nothing rounded and nothing drifting, a pixel 3000 along as exact as
	one at the corner. A radial carries its u so, its root `:sys_math
	:q_sqrt`, within one step. Antialiasing is `:fpoly`'s as before, and a
	gradient of one color draws what that color does to the byte. A child
	of a farm of strips sets a paint on its own canvas of the shared
	pixels. `tests/system/test_paint.lisp`.

*	The gradients take a matrix: `(. canvas :set_linear x y x1 y1 stops
	[spread m])` and `(. canvas :set_radial cx cy r stops [spread m])`, m
	a mat3x2 from the space they are given in to the canvas's pixels, as
	an SVG's is, under which a linear's bands lie at a slant to its line
	and a radial is an ellipse. Both spans are one form: two rows of a
	matrix from a pixel to the gradient's own space, 32 bits of fraction,
	worked out again for the first pixel of every run and stepped along
	it, the inverse made once in doubles. A linear is no longer exact to
	the integer but within one step of the 256, a straight ramp still
	exact; 8% quicker, the radial 7% slower.

## VP

*	A canvas's span, the function each run of a fill ends in, is a field
	of it, `+canvas_span`, with `+canvas_span_data` its own: `:fpoly`,
	`:fbox` and `:span` call through it, `:span_noclip` unless a paint is
	set, `gui/canvas/paint.vp`. They keep nothing in a register over the
	call but `:r0`, so a span may trash all else. `cmd/trace.lisp` has
	`+pointer_families`: a call through a pointer that names a method
	takes what all of its family trash, as a virtual method's call does
	its overrides, and `lint` is clean. Times, `bench_fill`: the M4 level
	but the soft discs, 3% slower; the Pi level or quicker; the x64's soft
	fills 3% to 16% slower, the box most, its hard ones level.
	`:span_noclip`'s x86 code is the same byte for byte and `:fpoly`'s is
	24 bytes shorter; not the call, the offsets or the order code is
	loaded in, each tried. Thought to be where `:fpoly`'s loops now lie on
	an Intel of that age, not proven; aligning loop heads in the x86
	translator is on the list.

*	`xml-parse` given what is not a stream took an arm64 node down,
	`Illegal instruction`, where it should have said `wrong_types`, and
	would have on RISC-V and LoongArch: the error path of its tokenizer,
	`lib/xml/lisp.vp`, came after the names of its states, 43 bytes, so
	its first instruction was 3 past a place of 4. The names are after
	the code now. It was also the `VP64 UNALIGNED lib/trans/vp64 +858`
	of every VP64 node that translated it, the translator writing that
	instruction at an odd address. `tests/text/test_xml.lisp`, the first
	test of the parser, has a document parsed and the wrong argument;
	`tests/system/test_code_place.lisp` looks through every function of
	`obj/vp/`, 1202, for code after data off a place of 4, and this was
	the one.
