# ChrysaLisp 7.8

## Install

*	A new `snapshot.zip`, its VP64 boot image, which an install starts
	from: `root.inc` binds `exp`, `log` and `tanh` of the class of a
	double, natives the image of 7.7 does not have, so an install from it
	stops at its start. 835 bytes bigger, the Windows host as it was.

*	A Raspberry Pi's host program is built again, `make`: a kernel on its
	GPU could run before a write given before it was over, see Apps below.
	The change is in what any host does on Vulkan, and is of no need but
	to that driver: a Mac's draws and computes as it did. The snapshot has
	the Windows host of it, 512 bytes bigger.

## Apps

*	The MNIST app is Digits, `apps/science/digits`: it is named for what
	it learns, as the next networks of `lib/nn` will be, and MNIST is the
	data set it learns them from, whose names keep it. Its settings are
	kept in `digits.tre`, the rate, batch and back end chosen under the
	old name are not carried over.

*	Digits learns by a net of the user's choice, the dense one it had or
	LeNet-5, a bar beside CPU and GPU, kept with the app's settings. The
	jobs of its farm and of its GPU task say which, and each keeps a
	model of each net it is given. On the M4's GPU LeNet-5 learns 5120
	samples a second, an epoch in 11s, 90.8% of the test set right after
	the first. The picture of a
	net shows each layer of convolutions or pooling as its planes, a
	tile each, a grey each number from the plane's least to its most,
	and the canvas of what the first layer looks for shows LeNet's six
	filters. A frame of LeNet's picture is drawn in 4ms.

*	Digits' GPU side is a farm of one task, `lib/task/jobs.inc`, as its CPU
	side is a farm of many: the one protocol, a step a job. A farm lasts
	while its work goes on; it is closed when it stops, at a reset and at
	a switch between the CPU and the GPU, and the next work makes a new
	one. Training started again after a pause of 20 seconds or more stood
	still for half a minute at a time: the children had gone, given
	nothing, and the farm gave them the next jobs. The GPU's task now goes by
	itself too, where it waited for ever if its app had gone. The dense
	net, a batch of 1,024 on the M4: 151,000 samples a second on the GPU,
	90,000 on the farm; LeNet-5, 5,120 on the GPU and 18,000 on the farm.

*	`nn-gpu` gives `:nil`, as for no GPU, for a model past what its
	kernels sum, a layer of more than 8,192 inputs say: a shader's loop
	goes to a constant, and a sum of more stopped short and said nothing.
	A batch bigger than the GPU model was made for is thrown for.

*	Surface draws its GPU frames by a farm of one task, as it draws its
	CPU frames by a farm of a task a node: the same tile job, each child
	writing the app's pixels in shared memory. `lib/gpu/shade.inc` is the
	GPU's side of `lib/gpu/tile.inc`, and `(shader-pixel-kernel)` makes a
	pixel shader a kernel, in the shader language itself, so the GPU is
	the node's own and its child on any node of the machine. The app no
	longer drives the GPU from its own task, in strips. 59.8 frames a
	second on the M4, where the GUI driver's drawing was 58.3. Raymarch
	makes its film the same way, by the one farm or the other, 40 frames
	of 600 by 600 in 4.4s on the GPU, its kernel's build in that, where
	the driver's drawing and a read back of its texture took 4.2s.
	Mandelbrot's GPU picture is a farm of one too, `gpu.lisp`, given the
	whole picture as its square, 4 to 5.4ms a picture on the M4. Its farm,
	either one, is closed when the picture has stood still for 2 seconds
	and made again by the next move, where it was kept for as long as
	the app ran.

*	`(gpu-read buffer offset len buf at)` reads a buffer of the GPU
	straight into a buf, a block of shared memory, from `at` on: the
	host copies what it read to its place, with no str made and no copy
	after. The GPU's farm children read a frame onto their app's pixels
	so. Mandelbrot's picture went from 5.3 to 7.1ms to 4 to 5.4ms on the
	M4; its copy had been a plot a pixel. The Raymarch film is the same
	bytes with it and without.

*	`(jobs-end jobs select task_idx reply_idx)`, `lib/task/jobs.inc`,
	lets a farm go when its work stops: its children are told to go and
	the app's two mailboxes for it are made new, so nothing late comes
	in. A farm lasts while its work goes on, and the next work makes a
	new one. Every app with a farm of `Jobs` does so now. Canvas, Bubbles
	and Opcodes had kept theirs while one task drew, and started every
	child again after; Planets and Mesh had judged by a time whether
	their children might have gone.

*	Triangles are drawn on the GPU by a farm of one, the same job as the
	CPU's farm of many, and the GUI driver's own shader path is gone.
	The GPU's child, `lib/gpu/tris_gpu_child.lisp`, finds for itself how
	its frame gets to the app: on the node that has the GUI it draws
	straight into a texture that is shown, nothing copied, and anywhere
	else to a target of its own, read back onto the app's shared pixels.
	An app holds a `(Render-tris canvas vfile pfile)`, `lib/gpu/
	render_farm.inc`, and has no farm, child or GPU call of its own:
	Planets, Model, Mesh and the Network Map are on it, each some 100
	lines shorter. A draw of a strip can have a pair of shaders of its
	own. A frame the GPU draws goes to a canvas that is not on show, and
	is exchanged for the one that is when it is whole.

*	Every app that draws by a farm holds a render of that one library,
	`docs/ai_digest/render_farm.md`, section 5. `(Render-shade canvas
	file)` is a pixel shader over a frame: on the node that has the GUI
	the GPU draws it straight into the texture that is shown, where it
	was read back before, so Surface has no copy of a frame at all;
	Raymarch keeps the read back, its film is of the pixels; Eyes draws
	only when the mouse has moved, by the GPU or in its own task.
	`(Render-work canvas child [gpu_child])` is an app's own children and
	jobs: Canvas, Bubbles, Opcodes and Mandelbrot, and Digits with no
	canvas, its work is not a picture. A GPU child answers when the GPU
	is done with a frame, not when it was handed over, so an app that
	gives the next on the answer can not flood a slow GPU. And a frame
	of a pixel shader is given to the GPU some rows at a time, sized to
	about 12ms of it, so on a slow GPU the desktop has its turn between:
	Surface on a Raspberry Pi 4 leaves the mouse live.

*	A kernel is given to the GPU a piece at a time too,
	`(shader-gpu-run-paced)`, each piece timed and the next sized to
	about 12ms: Mandelbrot's picture was one run of its kernel, and on a
	Raspberry Pi 4 the mouse waited for it. Its CPU picture is shown
	filling in only when the view stands still.

*	A desktop that quits is gone at once. Its node waited half a second
	for the nodes it started to go, whether or not they had, with the
	window still up and the mouse still: five seconds on the emulator.
	It waits for them to go.

*	The Motion engine does work for what moves. A tick passes over a
	figure with no component to run, keeping the order it comes to
	them in while the tree is as it was, and a figure is placed when
	the draw or a meeting asks where it is, `(. rostrum :world figure)`,
	not each tick. A whole scene run with no picture is 2.5 times
	quicker, every figure's state the same at every step looked at. A
	figure is told to go by `(. figure :kill)`, not a `(def)` of
	`:fig_dead`. The Motion app's seek bar has a solid elevator till it
	can be used.

*	A window of Mesh or of the Network Map that is sized no longer
	flickers or stalls. The size wanted is kept, a canvas of it made
	when the next frame is given, and put on show when that frame is
	whole.

*	The host has a new GPU table and GUI table, so a new `snapshot.zip`,
	and every machine's host is built again by `make`. The GPU table
	has pairs, meshes, pictures, targets and a draw. The GUI table has
	lost eight calls, its shaders, meshes, pairs and draws, and kept
	the texture the GPU can draw into. `(. canvas :shade)`, `(. canvas
	:shade_tris)`, the `canvas-shader-`, `canvas-pair-` and
	`canvas-mesh-` functions and `lib/gpu/gui.inc` are gone;
	`(canvas-target canvas)` is new. `(Jobs)` takes an `away` of `:here`,
	children kept on the app's own node.

*	`docs/ai_digest/render_farm.md` is how an app draws, or works numbers
	out, on the CPU or the GPU: the one model, the life of a farm, shared
	pixels, the kinds of job the apps have, and a skeleton of an app and
	of a child to start from.

*	A JPEG is read a row of MCUs to a native call, where it was a call
	or two a block, driven from Lisp: every block of every component of
	a row of a baseline scan, its codes and its pixels, and of a
	progressive one its codes, each scan, and its pixels at the end. The
	inverse DCT takes only the rows of a block that have a number, and of
	each only as far as its last, the same sums. The pixels are the same
	to the byte, 17 pictures on three machines. The Gun's picture in
	Model, 2048 by 2048 and progressive, is read in 160ms on an M4 where
	it took 660ms, and a baseline one of 768 by 768 in 23ms, from 39ms.

*	Model draws with no GPU as Planets and Mesh do, a strip of the frame
	by each node of the machine, where it had drawn every frame in its
	own task. The parts of a model are the draws of a strip, their
	pictures in shared memory, the one copy of each for all the nodes. A
	line under its status says how the frames are drawn and how long one
	took: a man of 5004 triangles and 5 materials in 2.1ms by 15 nodes
	of an M4. `(shader-strip-picture canvas)`, `lib/gpu/tris.inc`, is a
	loaded picture's pixels in shared memory, for a farm's children.

*	A kernel runs on the GPU in groups of 256 threads, not 64. On a
	Raspberry Pi 4 the time of a kernel goes as one over the size of its
	groups: the Surface shader's frame as a kernel went from 1.2s to
	0.57s, and Surface on its GPU from 0.8 frames a second to 1.6. On
	Metal it makes no difference.

*	A kernel on a Raspberry Pi's GPU waits for the writes given before
	it. Its Vulkan driver runs kernels on one queue of the hardware and
	copies on another, and forgets, from one submit to the next, that a
	kernel is to wait for a copy: with other programs on the GPU a kernel
	could run on what a buffer held before the write, and a model of
	`lib/nn` worked out the loss of the sample before, about one session
	in four of four tasks on the GPU at once. On Vulkan each dispatch now
	begins with a small copy of its own, which the driver does have it
	wait for, 70us a dispatch on a Pi 4. 35 such sessions with none
	wrong, 7 of 31 before.

*	Surface gives its GPU the next frame as soon as one is answered,
	when that took a tick or more, and Mandelbrot its next picture: an
	Intel MacBook Pro draws Surface at 45 to 50 frames a second, where
	waiting for the tick made it 30. Surface's rate is now from frame to
	frame; on a GPU slower than two frames a second it read 0.0 between
	them.

*	Digits started again, or stepped by hand, at the end of an epoch no
	longer stops on an error: a step for which the epoch has no batch
	left is the epoch's end, its check of the part kept back, however the
	step is asked for. It was so only as it trained on, so a pause in an
	epoch's last step, or a bigger batch chosen near its end, took the
	next step past the end of the samples. And the loss of a sample on
	the GPU is never infinity: a chance too small for a float gives the
	loss of the least chance, 85.2, as on the CPU. The kernel's least
	chance was a number of 7 places, which a shader reads as 0.

*	What an app downloads is kept in `downloads/` at the top of the
	tree, a folder for each thing fetched, which git does not track:
	Digits keeps MNIST in `downloads/mnist/`, for any app that wants it,
	and Model each file of a model it fetches in `downloads/models/`, by
	its address, so a model picked again comes from there: the Spider, its
	`.obj`, `.mtl` and five pictures, took 3.2 s the first time, 6 ms the
	next.
	A tree that pulls this keeps the data set it fetched before in
	`apps/science/mnist/data/`, which git did not track either, and the
	app fetches it again: the old folder can be deleted.

*	Surface and Raymarch shade their CPU frames by a herd on the nodes of
	this machine, a child a node, kept off the window's own node. Their
	farms had started children on any node of the network: with another
	machine in it, a Pi or an x64, its tiles sent their pixels back by
	mail where those here shade straight into the shared ones, and every
	frame waited for its last tile, 5 s a frame of Surface on the M4 with
	the x64 and a Pi in the network. A tile is one long call of native
	code, and kept off the window's node it no longer holds the window up
	while it runs, nor while the shader is first assembled.

## Lisp

*	`(double-to-str)` of an infinity or a NaN gives `nan`. It never
	came back from one: it took the tens out of an infinity for ever.

*	`(double-to-str)` of a 0 with its sign set, the `neg` of a 0 or the
	loss of a certain guess from a GPU, gives `0.0`. It never came back
	from that either: `(=)` is bit for bit, so it was not the 0 looked
	for, and ten times it is no nearer 1.

*	`(tanh num)`, of a fixed, a double or a float, and `tanh` of the
	shader language, on every back end. Of `:sys_math :d_tanh` and
	`:f_tanh`, each worked out at its own width, a single's in 32 bits by
	`:f_exp`, the first function of `sys/math` of singles: below 0.55
	the series of e to the twice of it less 1, to the 19th power of a
	double and the 11th of a single, and above, by exp. Of 858 numbers
	from -25 to 25 each is at the most 2 units of its last place from the
	true tanh, 680 of the doubles and 684 of the singles the nearest. The
	VP back end of the shader language calls `:f_tanh` as it is, so a
	kernel's tanh there is Lisp's to the bit, and the GPU's own is near
	it. A fixed's is by `:d_tanh`, to its 16 bits.

*	The classes of numbers are a ladder of what each can do, `:num`,
	`:fixed`, `:double`, `:float`, and of vectors `:nums`, `:fixeds`,
	`:doubles`, `:floats`: a float comes of a double, as every float is
	exactly a double, and floats of doubles, where both came of fixed
	point. Fixed point keeps what drawing works in, `frac` `floor` `ceil`
	`recip` `sin` `cos`; `exp`, `log` and `tanh` are of a double and a
	float only, an error of a fixed, and `:sys_math :q_exp` and `:q_log`,
	of no other use, are gone. `(double? x)` is true of a float, as
	`(fixed? x)` of a double is; `(double?? x)` asks what x is exactly,
	and what reads a double as stored asks that, the VP functions of
	`lib/nn` and `lib/media` taking exactly `:DOUBLE` and `:DOUBLES`.
	`docs/lisp/numerics.md` is the one document of numbers now, the
	digest's merged into it.

*	A model of `lib/nn` is of a kind, `(nn-model description :floats)`,
	doubles if not said: its params, and every vector and constant its
	sums meet, are of it, its loss a double whatever the kind.
	`nn-scale-bytes` fills a doubles or a floats. Digits trains in floats
	on the CPU as on the GPU, its params and gradient bufs half the
	bytes. A sample forward and back takes 68us in floats, 69 in doubles:
	the time is the Lisp of a row at a time, not the memory. tanh of a
	layer is `(tanh)`, where it was a sum of exp.

*	`nn-model` throws for a description it took and could not teach: an
	activation that is none, `:rleu`, which trained as no activation at
	all, softmax in a layer before the last, a last layer without it, and
	a block wider than its planes.

*	`nums-scale` and `nums-add-scaled` take vectors of the one class, the
	one written to as well, as the other `nums-` ops do: of two kinds is
	an error. They took any, and a doubles' sums over a floats, whose
	numbers are half the size, read and wrote past its end.

*	The natives of a convolutional network, in `lib/nn`, each of exactly a
	doubles or a floats, and the floats' sums of singles, as a GPU's:
	`nn-gemm`, a matrix times a matrix, either given turned, added into
	its output or not, with a bias for each row, the three products of a
	layer of convolutions, forward, its weights' gradient and its error
	back; `nn-im2col`, the patches of planes as the columns of a matrix,
	of any stride and padding, and `nn-col2im`, those patches added back;
	`nn-pool` and `nn-pool-back`, the averages of blocks, and an error
	spread back over them. Each is the same to the bit as its sums in
	Lisp in the same order. `nn-gemm` does 3.5 to 4 thousand multiplies
	and adds a microsecond on the M4; where each number is a dot of two
	rows it takes four rows of b side by side, four sums at once each in
	its own order, 5 thousand, where one at a time was 1.8.

*	A description of `lib/nn` takes conv and pool layers: `(:input 1 28
	28)`, `(:conv 6 5 :tanh :pad 2)`, `(:pool 2)`, so LeNet-5 is a list
	of eight, 61706 numbers as the papers count them. A conv layer's
	weights are one row of all its filters in the params, its sums the
	patches, the filters times them and back by the natives, a pool's by
	the average natives. The gradients of both are the slope of the loss
	in test_nn, of padding, strides and an edge left over, and a conv net
	of floats gives what one of doubles does. The GPU's model takes them
	too, `lib/nn/shaders`: a conv layer convolved where it is, a thread an
	output, an input back and a weight to move, a pool layer an average
	and its error spread back; test_nn_gpu has a model of each against the
	CPU's, padding, pooling, a stride and a ReLU among them. A dense
	layer's tanh on the GPU is `(tanh)`. ReLU, sigmoid and tanh and
	their slopes are natives too, `nn-act` and `nn-act-slope`, to the bit
	as the Lisp they were: of LeNet's first layer the Lisp tanh and slope
	took 570us of a sample, its gemm 29. A sample of LeNet-5 in floats,
	forward and back, is 374us, forward 173. Weights start by He's uniform
	for a ReLU, LeCun's for the rest.

*	`+float_0` to `+float_10`, the negatives and the fractions, and
	`+float_pi` `+float_hpi` `+float_2pi`, beside the doubles' of
	`lib/math/vector.inc`, made by `def_nums_consts`. The pi constants
	are as near pi as each kind holds it: `+double_pi` had been the
	fixed point pi made a double, 5 places.

*	A function that ignores what it is given is `(lambda (&ignore) ...)`,
	of any number of arguments, and the tree's are so, where many were
	`(lambda (_) ...)`, which binds a `_`, or `(lambda (&) ...)`, which
	takes exactly one. The quick bind of a call takes a last `&ignore`,
	the names before it bound and the values after left, where any `&`
	had sent a call to the general bind: a million calls of `(lambda
	(&ignore) 1)` by `map` take 10ms, of `(lambda (_) 1)` 12, of `(lambda
	(&) 1)` 14.5.

## Tools

*	`rack -e` runs each session on the emulator and `rack -n num` gives
	it that many nodes. `rack -e -n 4 -g` is a hidden desktop about as
	slow as a Raspberry Pi 4, on a fast machine: the test of what a
	slow machine shows, an app that holds the GUI's node, a wait that
	is seconds there. It does not make the GPU slow.

*	`check` reads `.shader` files, for two rules of its 42, `bracket` and
	a new one, `places`: a number written with more than 4 places after
	its point, which a shader rounds to 4, so `0.00001` is 0. One that
	needs more is written as a str.

*	`make clean`, which `make install` starts with, `install.bat` and the
	install of `rack.ps1` take away the manifests of the VP64's code,
	`obj/make_vp64_VP64` and its `_names`, with the code. Left, they said
	the code they had just taken away was made, so a plain `make apps`
	made none of the VP64's apps after an install, and the emulator's
	`test_source_bin` failed till `make all apps` was run.

## VP

*	`:sys_math :f_sin`, `:f_cos` and `:f_log`, beside `:f_exp` and
	`:f_tanh`: of a single, given back as the nearest single, or within a
	bit of it. sin and cos bring the angle to the turn and sum their
	series in doubles, and round once: of 871 numbers every cosine is the
	nearest single and all but one sine, cos 0 is 1. A float's sin cos
	exp log and tanh are by them, in 32 bits, and the shader compiler
	calls them as they are, no double made of the single and back, so a
	kernel's maths on the VP is Lisp's of a float to the bit. The frames
	of Surface and Raymarch on the VP take the same time, their maths is
	little of their work. `:q_sin` and `:q_cos`, the fixed point ones the
	arcs of drawing use, are by `:f_sin` and `:f_cos` too: a single holds
	an angle of a drawing, to 256 radians, to all 16 bits of a fixed's
	fraction. A fixed sin or cos can come one 1/65536 from what it was,
	where the single's rounding crosses where it is cut to 16 bits, so
	the symbol fonts, `fonts/Symbols*.ctf`, are made again by `symbols`,
	3 to 12 bytes of each, and the diagrams with arcs.

*	`assign` makes no conversion between a single and a double: a 32 bit
	field to a double register, or a single to a double and back, throws,
	where it had put in a `vp-cvt-fd` or `vp-cvt-df` of its own. The few
	places that leant on it, a floats' sum and dot, a float's maths done
	as a double, pow, and n2i n2q n2d of a float, write their loads and
	conversions, the VP and native code made as it was to the bit.
	`(vp-reg-single dreg)` names the single of a double's register.

*	A value not wanted is `&`, where it had been `_`: a destination of
	`assign`, so the outputs of a call, `(call :obj :type '(:r0) '(&
	:r1))`, and `_` there is refused; the wildcard of `lmatch?`'s pattern;
	a place of a `bind` or of a lambda's parameters, and `&ignore` for the
	rest, `&rest _` gone. Over 600 places of the VP sources and 120 of the
	Lisp, the VP made the same but for `lmatch?`. A `_` that is read stays
	a name: the error `catch` binds, a macro's body spliced as `~_`, the
	view a `ui-` form with no name is made in. The first build on an old
	boot image matches no `&` of the optimizer's patterns, the second, on
	its own, does.

*	The functions of `sys/math` of a number, `d_` `f_` and `q_`, a value
	in a register in and out, keep to one set of what they may trash,
	`:r0` to `:r2` and `:d0` to `:d3`, `sys/math/trashes.inc`, so what
	calls them, the shader compiler and the classes of numbers, keeps what
	it wants in any other register across the call. `trace -l`, and so
	`lint`, says of one that goes outside it. `:d_pow`, which trashed
	`:d0` to `:d7`, keeps to it now: the double waits in `:d3` and the
	whole part of the power in `:r2`, the 1 of the roots in `:d3`, so
	none of it is memory, every power the same to the bit, 1226 of them
	compared. The shader compiler spills that one set around a call of
	`sys/math`, where it had two sets of its own and spilled every
	register around `pow`: of the 21 shaders of the tree, 170 moves to
	and from the stack are 148, Eyes and the atom of Molecule none.
