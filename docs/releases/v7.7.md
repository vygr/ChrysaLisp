# ChrysaLisp 7.7

## Apps

*	Text shows in a Vdu again, the Terminal, Viewer and Editor and the
	code of a Docs page: in 7.6 they were blank. Its configure no longer
	kept the width and height it was given, so it held no characters to
	load or draw.

*	In Films, Next at the end of the last film of a folder goes round to
	the first. It had stopped there, and the film shown at first is the
	last of its folder.

*	The VP of 64 files is indented by its blocks again: the checks that
	were in an `(errorcase)` were left at the margin, or a tab short in a
	loop, when it went in 7.6.

*	The Pcb app routes again: a route stopped at its start with an error,
	the bar's colour set and then asked to be drawn, as if `def` gave
	back the view and not the colour it set.

*	Motion has a third scene, Build: one function, `:list :cat`, from its
	source to a call of it. Its macros open into VP, the VP text of its
	bundle, the six translators and the size each makes, its arm64 code
	byte by byte with its header and links, the boot image of 238
	functions and the binding of it, and Lisp binding `lisp_cat` and the
	call going through the list vtable's slot. Every name, number and
	byte is the tree's.

*	Motion's scenes can be sought. A bar under the toolbar goes to any
	point of Story or Build, forward or back, playing or paused, and a
	scene played to its end plays again the same. Balls is played with,
	and is not.

*	The Raymarch film is made in `raymarch.flm.tmp` beside the film, which
	`.gitignore` keeps out of git and the herd's sync, where it was made in
	`obj/`, which is for code.

## Lisp

*	`(clear [idx] array ...)`: with an index first, each array is cut
	there, what is after it let go of, the capacity as it was and nothing
	copied. The index is as a slice's, so `(clear -4 a)` lets the last 3
	go, and one before the start or past the end is no error: all of it,
	or none. `:array :truncate` does it, the list's letting go of the
	elements as `:clear` does.

*	Sets and maps `:empty` with a count: about so many items let go of,
	`:t` while there are more. A storage member's plan made anew is a
	million blocks and more, and the old one let go of at once held the
	service 17.7 ms; it is let go of 1024 at a time with the plan's other
	work now, and the longest stat while a plan is made anew is 4.4 ms,
	from 20.1.

*	The first count of an exFAT volume's free clusters, made by the first
	write after it is opened, is a long of the bitmap at a time: 1.9 ms
	for a store of 5 GB in 4 KB clusters, where it was 68.5 ms a bit at a
	time, and the first put to a store after its open was 74 ms.

*	A store reads its `blocks/` folder as it is opened, not when the first
	block is read or put. Of a million blocks it has 4000 folders in it,
	7.3 ms to read and more as the store grows, and it was the most of
	the longest step of the first plan, 8.9 ms, 4.2 ms now.

*	A storage sweep is done a step at a time, 2 ms at a time when nothing
	else is asked, as the plan is, and answered when it is done. The blocks
	it goes through are the names the store holds, not a read of every
	folder. A block put while it is under way is spared, and a path set
	meanwhile is gone through before any more is let go of, so an object
	whose blocks were put before the sweep began and whose path was set
	during it keeps them. Of a store of a million blocks, the sweep was
	8.6 s, and a stat asked meanwhile waited 8.6 s; it is 5 s, and the
	longest a stat waited 7 ms, the longest step 3.8 ms.

*	A store's map of the blocks it keeps is made as big as it will ever
	need to be as the store is opened: a bucket for every 16 blocks it
	has, and no fewer than one for every 128 clusters of its volume, the
	most blocks there can be. It doubled as it grew, and of a million
	blocks the store was always full as it was opened: the first new
	block put after an open was 132 ms, 5 ms now. A find of a block not
	kept is 1.3 us at the most, 0.6 at 32 to a bucket.

*	A check of a store's blocks is done a step at a time too, a folder of
	them and then each block read, and answered when it is done. A block
	let go of since its folder was listed is not called wrong; a file of
	a block not known to be kept is, and a mend removes the file itself.
	Of a store of a million blocks it is 36 s, as it was, but other
	requests are answered meanwhile: the longest a stat waited was 5.4
	ms, where it waited the whole 36 s.

*	`range` is gone. It made a list of every number before anything was
	done with them: `(each f (range 0 size))` over 8 MB was a list of
	8 million numbers first. The `...!` iterators given no seqs, `'()`,
	count instead, from the start to the end, the end not done, and
	`(!)` is each number: `(each! (# (print (!))) '() 0 5)`. The numbers
	are as given, negative ones too, not indexes into a seq, and a start
	past the end counts down, as over a seq: `'() 5 0` is 4 down to 0.
	The start and end not given are 0. `(map! (const !) '() a b)` is a list of them where one is the
	thing wanted. A step is a count of them, `(* (!) 4)`, or a `while`
	when it is known only at run time. Each of the 835 uses in the tree
	is one of these now, and where a function looked up one seq at
	`(!)` it is given that seq to go along.

*	`(read-char stream width)` of a positive width, and `read-short`,
	`read-int` and the like, sign extend by the top bit of the last byte
	read. They went by its lowest bit: `read-short` of `ff 80` gave 33023,
	and of `00 01` gave -65280.

*	`lib/motion/` has effects: a halo, `:fig_glow`, a fill that goes to a
	second colour down a figure, `:fig_fill2`, a trail of the places it
	was, `:fig_trail`, a stroke of dashes that march, `:fig_dash`, and
	words that come a letter at a time, `:fig_reveal`. `Shake` jolts an
	attribute and dies away, `Emit` makes things at a rate, and
	`outline-mono` sets code in cells as a Vdu does, so it lines up in
	columns: a font keeps its glyphs by their ink.

*	A Motion rostrum goes on in steps of a sixtieth of a second, as many as
	the wall clock owes, `:advance`, with random numbers of its own from a
	seed, `:random`: a scene made again from its seed and gone on by as
	many steps is the same scene. `(. rostrum :copy)` makes all of it
	anew, a place to go on from, so a seek steps on from the nearest copy.

*	`(path-glow paths radii join)` gives the halos round a shape, a set of
	polygons for each radius, to be filled by the none zero rule. The
	Opcodes demo and Motion's `:fig_glow` both draw theirs with it.

*	Every set and map, `Fset`, `Fmap`, `Lset`, `Lmap`, `Xset`, `Xmap` and
	`Emap`, has `:some` beside `:each`: each key, or key and value, till
	the lambda gives other than `:nil`, and that is what it gives. A walk
	of a set of a million that wants the first few stops there.

## Tools

*	A member's plan of what it is to keep among the others is kept up to
	date, not made again after every write. A path set adds its object to
	it; a delete or a replace marks it to be made anew, and it is used as
	it is meanwhile, safe: what it says is mine is mine or was, and a
	block that becomes mine is taken from what is offered to others as it
	is added. The service works at it 2 ms at a time, when nothing is
	asked, in the apps' band, and a share answers with what there is. On a
	store of a million blocks a share after a write was 13 s, every
	request waiting; it is 1 ms, and a stat asked while a plan is made
	waits 6 ms at most. A share gives the 64 it sends, not all of them.

*	The storage service does what has come by its kind, not as it came:
	what an asker waits on to read first, then writes, then what the
	members ask of each other, and its own work when nothing is asked.
	One that has waited 20 ms goes first, whatever its kind. A stat sent
	behind 256 block puts waited 67 ms for all of them, and is answered
	in 2 ms.

*	`rack.sh` keeps what says its member is up, and that it follows, new
	in `/tmp`, and puts it back if it was taken: what is old there is
	deleted by the system, Ubuntu's after 10 days, the Mac's after 3, and
	a machine that followed stopped looking for commits and said nothing.

*	`make install`, `install.bat` and the install of `rack.sh` and
	`rack.ps1` keep to themselves, as `-a`: an install no longer joins the
	mesh. With the member stopped for an update, the install took its port,
	became the machine's member, and started the machine's store under the
	emulator while it built.

*	`make install`, `make clean`, `install.bat` and the update of
	`rack.ps1` take away `obj/vp64/VP64` before they unpack the snapshot.
	A node makes its own code of a function it binds, from the function's
	VP, and keeps it there for the next to load, and the installer, which
	the emulator runs, had kept the unzip and sum functions of installs
	before. The snapshot's VP64 image has the ops renumbered since 7.6:
	code of the old numbers, loaded as it was found, took the emulator
	down as `waypoint` read `.git`, at the end of every install of a tree
	that had been installed before 7.7, where a fresh clone installed.

*	The images of a storage scene, `storage -t`, and the picture font of
	`symbols -p` are made in `tests/scratch/`, where they were made in
	`obj/`, which is for code.

*	`storage -C -v` says, after a mend, that what was wrong was mended, all
	of it or how much, where it said the faults were there still: a cluster
	two have, a chain cut short and one not of the volume it only says.

*	`fmt` is a layout engine: it reads a file into its forms and comments
	and lays them out afresh in the house style, so however a file was
	laid out, the same forms give the same text. What is kept of the
	source is its comments, where a blank line was, strings, the help
	text of a command, and what a `;check off` covers. A form that fits
	is on its line, and the line is 120 columns, `-l`, for Lisp and VP
	alike. A form with a test or a body has a width of its own, however
	much room the line has, so the left edge shows the blocks: a `(when)`
	or a loop is a line only up to half the limit, an `(unless)` or a
	`(lambda)` two thirds, an `(if)` `(and)` `(or)` `(each)` three
	quarters, a call, a binding or a list the whole of it, and the test
	of a block that is broken, an `(and)` or `(or)` wider than half the
	limit, has a condition to a line; a definition, a `(cond)`, a `(when)` or a `(lambda)`
	of more than one body form, is broken by its rule; a name and its
	value, and a keyword and its value, are not parted; a form that must
	be over more than one line starts one of its own, and what comes
	after a form over more than one line starts a line. One that is only
	too long hangs on its line, as a `(lambda)` always does, if its first
	argument fits there and no other argument of the form will be over
	more than one line: then each starts a line. A line far in still has
	half the limit past its indent, so code deep in is not made a narrow
	column. Where the tree's authors had a habit it is the rule: the
	clause of `:t` has its first form beside it, a tab in as the rest
	are, an `(if)` that does not fit has its then and else each on a
	line, a `(#)` of two forms that fits is one line, an `(and)` of
	nothing but `(defq)`s has each on a line under the `(and)`, and a
	definition that fits is a line among others, one after another with
	no blank line between, a table of one line definitions as
	`lib/asm/vp.inc` has, where one that stands alone has its body on a
	line of its own. A table,
	a quoted list of atoms or of lists, an `(enum)` or `(bit)`, or a form
	of eight or more atoms, is laid out in rows of its cells, a name and
	its value, a form, or an atom, a power of two of them to a row, the
	same in each part of it, and a part that fits in one row is one row;
	a `(scatter (Fmap) ...)` of names and values is one, and a column, a
	cell to a row, is all under its head. A map of keys and the
	functions they are for, an event map as a key map, is a key and its
	action to a line however much room there is:
	`docs/reference/keys.md` is made the same to the byte.
	The source scanners take a `(` at the very start of a line to open a
	form at the top of a file: each form at the top starts its line and
	every line of what is in one is at least a tab in, so they find what
	they did, and a VP instruction, an `(import)` or an `(ffi)` inside a
	form is laid out as any form is. A declaration is never broken. The
	`includes` command reads a line at a time: a `(call`, `(entry`,
	`(exit`, `(jump` or the like that names a class always starts a
	line, and no line starts with such a word that is not one.
	`fmt -l 0` and `fmt -i` are gone. The `symbols` command writes
	`lib/consts/symbols.inc` as `fmt` has it. The tree itself is not
	laid out by it: `fmt` is set aside for now, and `lint` no longer ends
	with a check of the layout of the source.

*	`check` has `def`: a method of what `def` or `set` gives, the last
	value set and not the env, `(. (def view :paper c) :dirty)`. The
	way round is `(def (. view :dirty) :paper c)`.

*	`make` goes by the content of files, not their ages. A source file has
	a key, the hash of it and all it includes, its VP is made again when
	the key is not the one its VP was made from, and a platform's code when
	it is not of the VP there is now and that platform's translator as it
	is now. A file touched and not changed makes nothing, a comment makes
	the file's VP again and no code, and a build in the other mode is put
	back by the next make with no `all`. `all` is for timing a build and
	for code deleted or damaged. An age was of a second or two and a file
	written again inside it looked unchanged. See vp_translation.md.

*	An install's first step, `make all boot` under the emulator, had gone
	from 1.2 s to 1.9 on the M4 with the keys, where every one of them is
	made at once and the emulator is slow at it. It is 1.2 to 1.35 again:
	a key is a fold of a number of each file, not the hash of a sorted
	text of them, the scan workers hash the files they read, and the
	translators' sources are not assembled in a farm of their own first.
	With the apps the install is 1.9 s where it was 2.5.

*	A tree built by a make of before there were keys, a commit checked out
	to look at and built, and then come back from, was taken for what the
	notes said: nothing was made, and the system was the old commit's.
	Each bundle and boot image is noted with the age of its file, and one
	that this make did not write is made again, with the code of it on
	every platform.

*	The keys of the tree walk what every file includes once, not once a
	file: 82 of the 93 files a source file is made of are the assembler's.
	An install's first step on a Pi 4, under the emulator, is 28.5 s where
	the walk for every file had made it 31 to 35.

*	A make with nothing to do is 3 ms on the M4 where it was 9, and 15
	before the keys; on a Pi 4 it was 36. Half of it
	was a walk of the whole tree for source files, 3,000 files listed to
	find 500. What each folder holds is kept with the folder's age, and
	only a folder whose age has moved is listed again, so a new file is
	still found by the next make wherever it is. The functions the tree
	makes are kept from one boot image to the next, 60 ms of a boot build
	on the Pi, and a bundle whose source file has gone is found by the
	manifest, not by listing the bundles.

*	`boot` makes no image that is already of the platform's code as it is:
	`make boot` with nothing changed made the image again, and
	`make platforms boot` all six. The id beside an image is worked out
	from the hashes make keeps, not by reading every source file again,
	and a change that makes no code writes a new id beside the image there
	is. `make all boot` makes the image whatever. An image is of the code
	of the system's files, so making the apps leaves it as it is.

*	Code that is no longer made is taken away: a function taken out of a
	file or named again, one only a validate build has, left its code under
	`obj/` for a node to go on binding. A function moved to another file
	keeps its code.

*	`make apps` makes the apps that have changed, where it made every one
	of them each time, and so took away the mark of an install.
	`make all apps` makes them all.

*	A make is one farm. A job is a source file, assembled once if it has
	to be and its code made for every platform that wants it, where each
	platform had a farm of its own, one after another, and each read the
	file again. The boot images are jobs of the same herd, made at once
	when the code they are of is in. `make all platforms boot` is 82 ms
	on the M4 where it was 148, `make platforms boot` after a change to a
	function 47 where it was 102, and the apps of an install 0.63 s where
	they were 0.93. An error in a file is reported once, not once for
	each platform.

*	A build's workers give way after each function, where a file, or a
	boot image, ran with no task switch: the other tasks of a worker's
	node, the links that pass mail through it among them, are not kept
	waiting for the length of a job. And a build has more workers than
	nodes where the nodes are few, one and three more for each node beyond
	the first, to no more than 20: one a node left a node or two of four
	with none, and a worker waiting for its next job had nothing to do.
	`make test` on the M4 on 4 nodes is 89 ms where it was 119, on 8 nodes
	55 where it was 70, and on 19 as it was. An emulated install on a Pi
	4, 28.5 s or 37.5 by where the workers fell, is 25.5 s or 29.

*	`make -v 1` ends with what it did: the source files read again and
	the files assembled, and for each platform the files code was made
	of and the functions that was.

## VP

*	A tree whose translators are still the vpb_ ones of before 7.6 no longer
	builds its way forward: `make install` brings it up, as from 7.5.

*	`vp-ddef` names the float registers as doubles, `:d0` on, what
	`vp-fdef` did, and `vp-fdef` names them as singles, `:f0` on. A single
	and its double are one register, and a trash of either is a trash of
	both.

*	vpx, the expression compiler of the VP, is gone, and with it
	`def-vars`, `push-scope`, `pop-scope` and `return`. A function's frame
	is a `(structure slot 0 ...)`, made by `(vp-alloc slot_size)` and
	freed by `(vp-free slot_size)`, its fields `(:rsp slot_x)`, many at a
	time by `load-fields` and `save-fields`, the width of each from its
	type as before. `vp-alloc` and `vp-free` round a size up to whole
	longs. The vpx documents are gone with it. Every function of the tree
	is written so, most with fewer instructions and less going to and from
	the stack than vpx gave: the kernel, the links, the loader, fonts,
	filled polygons, stroking, the image and stream codecs.

*	No pass keeps the slots of a frame in registers: `lib/asm/slots.inc`
	is gone, with what each call noted for it, `method-trashes` and
	`path-method`. A slot is memory, as written, and `vpopt.inc` is the
	only pass over a function's instructions. The pass had changed 42 of
	1,074 functions, 217 stack loads and 74 stores less for 375 register
	copies, 85 instructions and 528 bytes of arm64 more, and it was a
	sixth of a build: `make test` on one node is 0.238 s, was 0.286 s, on
	19 nodes 0.037 s, was 0.042 s. A value wanted again between calls is
	held in a register by the source.

*	31 of those 42 functions are written by hand to do what the pass
	looked for: a value that lives over calls is in a register those
	calls are declared to leave, a call's own result is used in place of
	a reload, and a frame is only what a call that keeps nothing is made
	over. They are 1,770 instructions with 155 stack loads and 108 stores,
	where the source as it was gave 1,958, 335 and 231, and the pass
	2,020, 198 and 181. `defq`, `setq`, the bind of a lambda's args, the
	number reader and the intern of a symbol are among them, with the
	vdu's draw, the film frame decoder, a font's textures, the path
	filters and the streams of Lisp functions. Nine have no frame at all
	now, and a font's `sym_texture` and `ascii_textures` none on their
	usual path. The arm64 code of the tree is 1,204 bytes less than with
	the pass.

*	The source says more in fewer statements, and loads in the order of
	the memory where that makes pairs. 184 assigns that stood next to
	another are one with it, 55 loads straight before a call are the
	call's operands and 14 entries take their slots: the VP of every
	function is the same but for one copy gone. 54 assigns have their
	operands in the order the fields lie in, three path frames are laid
	in the order their calls load them, and the PCB router, the curve
	generators and the init of an error load in order: 39 more pairs on
	arm64. An ordering sort in `assign` itself was measured, 3 to 4% of a
	build, and not kept. `docs/ai_digest/evidence_not_faith.md` has the
	measurements of all of it.

*	Every method has its `entry`, and an `exit` at each return, against
	the registers its `class.inc` declares: 59 methods had no entry and
	68 no exit, or one missing. The callbacks inside functions have theirs
	by the callback's own declaration, and the local subroutines reached by
	`(call 'label ...)` theirs in the local form. Where a copy of an input, or to an
	output, stood next to one the `entry` or `exit` does it. An `entry`
	takes an input only to where the code after it reads it: 35 had taken
	one to a slot or another register while the code went on reading the
	declared register, which a change to the declaration would have
	broken. They name the registers the code reads, and the move is an
	`assign` after them. The VP of every function is the same.

*	The `errorif-lisp-args-len`, `-sig`, `-match` and `-type` checks of a
	Lisp binding are `gotoif-lisp-args-...`, named as `gotoif` is for what
	they do, and take the args list as a register or as a symbol of one
	that `(vp-rdef)` made: 56 functions that name it say `args`.

*	Functions of a validate build and of a normal one mix: `make validate`
	with no `boot`, validate functions on a normal boot image, printed
	`Heap overrun !` and did not start. How a heap sets out its cells, a
	validate one keeps a guard word after each, was known to every function
	that frees a cell in line. Now only `:sys_heap :init`, `:alloc` and
	`:free` know it, `:free` is a function, every boot image holds the three,
	and a validate function calls it where a normal one frees in line. The
	heap's checks are the boot image's, the rest each function's own.

*	No function is made by a validate build alone. `:sys_task :stack`,
	`:obj :dead`, `:sys_heap :overrun` and `:double_free`, and
	`:sys_mem :bad_free`, what a validate check jumps to, are made in
	every build and called in one. A node that bound a validate function
	before they were there stopped, `Bind error: sys/task/stack`, and a
	normal boot image could not make them from the VP: a first validate
	build worked by the order its files happened to be written in.

*	`assign` throws for memory to memory and a constant to memory, where a
	long of memory to memory had given no instruction at all.

*	A field named `size` of a structure, or `fields` of a frame, throws: it
	is the structure's own name, and was lost to it. The loader's `vp_find`
	had one, and a bind of a function with no native, found from its VP,
	wrote past its frame.

*	`vp-lnot-rr`, `vp-land-rr`, `vp-ext-rr` and the sets, `vp-seq-cr` to
	`vp-sge-rr`, are gone: only vpx made them. Of each translator, the
	VP64 emulator and its translator, their code went with them. An op's
	number is its place in `lib/asm/ops.inc`, so single float ops, which no
	translator's own code uses, took the places they left, and the
	translators make themselves as before. VP64's ops are renumbered, in
	groups now: integer of a constant, of a register, compare and branch,
	memory, calls and jumps, doubles, singles, one list in
	`src/host/vp64.cpp` that `lib/trans/vp64.vp` follows. A VP64 image
	from before does not run on the new host, the new `snapshot.zip` has
	one that does, and a tree that builds its way forward wants
	`make apps` as well as `make all boot platforms`: a VP64 app object
	is made again when its source changes, not its translator.

*	The VP64 emulator's ops each end in a jump to the next of their own,
	in place of one switch, but on Apple Silicon, where the switch is
	the faster, `VP64_SWITCH` in the Makefile. Of the same Lisp, the x64
	Mac 9% faster and the Pi 30%. clang made it a switch again, one
	jump, till `-mllvm -tail-dup-pred-size` and `-tail-dup-succ-size`
	let it copy a block of 182 ways; gcc needs nothing.

*	A shader's native function is named by the translator of its CPU and
	`lib/asm/ops.inc` as well as by the back end, so one kept from before
	a change to them is not run: a VP64 node ran one of the old numbers
	and went down.
